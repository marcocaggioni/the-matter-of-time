Exhibition Panels

A comprehensive exploration of time through 20 carefully curated panels

Panel 1: What is time, then?

The theme of the New York Encounter this year invites us to look at our need for a place where our humanity is welcomed and nurtured in its truth. We had the desire to look at time, one of the fundamental dimensions of our human experience. We want to explore it, to be able to discover what it is and be helped to recognize where it is approached according to its real nature.

Panel 2: What is time, then?

It is difficult for us to explain what time is, yet in the way we approach many life situations we have unspoken yet strong expectations about time, its role and its value. In today's fast-paced world, where instant results often seem ideal, time is often seen as something to manage, endure, or conquer rather than a gift to embrace.

Panel 3: The Experience of Time

How did we discover time? Words are names of experiences so if we want to understand the word, we need to understand what experience it is connected to. Here we list three different experiences the word time is connected to: the experience of dividing, the experience of dealing with a precious and finite resource, and connection to motion.

Panel 4: TIME AND THE COSMOS

How did we come to know time? We started to count and divide it based on the observation of the "cosmos": the order around us. Many periodic, cyclical events that we can observe suggested how to make sense of time. Our ancestors spent a lot of time looking at the sky and their experience was that everything rotated and repeated around them.

Panel 5: From the Circle to the Arrow of Time

Many ancient civilizations viewed time as a cyclical phenomenon where the universe repeats in endless loops. It is the Judeo-Christian tradition that introduced a linear conception of history. St. Augustine formally argued in the 4th century that time has a beginning at the moment of Creation.

Panel 6: The Pendulum

Galileo Galilei made a groundbreaking discovery when he noticed a chandelier swaying overhead and observed, timing it with his pulse, that it always took the same time to complete its swing. This surprise discovery led to the invention of the pendulum clock by Christiaan Huygens in 1657, revolutionizing timekeeping.

Panel 7: THE HISTORY OF TIME MEASUREMENT

Our ability to measure time increasingly accurately has improved dramatically over the centuries. From pendulum clocks to marine chronometers, from quartz oscillators to atomic clocks, each innovation has refined our understanding and precision of time measurement.

Panel 8: THE DIRECTION OF TIME

The fundamental laws of physics are symmetric with respect to time. Yet our experience tells us that time flows in one direction. The Second Law of Thermodynamics, postulated by R. Clausius in 1865, is the first physics law which embeds the direction of time in its definition.

Panel 9: TIME at the EXTREMES - SPECIAL RELATIVITY

The finite speed of light implies that we always see into the past. Einstein discovered that the speed of light is always the same, introducing Special Relativity and the concept of time dilation—the same event has different duration depending on the observer's motion.

Panel 10: TIME and GRAVITY - GENERAL RELATIVITY

Space and time are not fixed but modified by energy and matter. Time is slower closer to a massive object. Gravitational time dilation needs to be accounted for GPS to work, and its effect is dramatic close to very massive objects such as black holes.

Panel 11: COSMIC TIMEKEEPING - RADIO PULSARS

Our galaxy is provided with its own set of clocks—radio pulsars. Pulsars are very dense collapsed stars that spin very quickly. Their regularity is such that in some cases they are more stable than atomic clocks, allowing us to look for modifications of spacetime induced by gravitational waves.

Panel 12: THE TIME of the COSMOS

Hubble's observations showed that our Universe is not fixed, but is expanding. We are able to observe the cosmic afterglow of the Big Bang—the Cosmic Microwave Background light from only 400 thousand years after the Big Bang, or 14.1 billion years ago.

Panel 13: THE TIME of the COSMOS

The formation of light elements took place in the first 20 minutes of the Universe. It then took 400 thousand years for the Universe to be cool enough for the existence of stable hydrogen atoms. The long time required for the first generation of stars was necessary to synthesize elements essential for life.

Panel 14: BIOLOGICAL EVOLUTION - TIME SHAPES LIFE

The evolution of life on Earth was a long process, which started about 4 billion years ago. Natural Selection is a mechanism in which individuals with beneficial traits produce more offspring. Life was shaped THROUGH time, and also shaped BY time—by our experience of time on Earth.

Panel 15: RHYTHMS IN NATURE

Organisms synchronize to nature's cycles. The most evident beneficial traits are circannual rhythms (yearly), circalunar rhythms (monthly), and circadian rhythms (daily). These rhythms are set by internal clocks synchronized to external environmental cues and are written in our DNA.

Panel 16: THE TIMES OF THE BRAIN

Every organism needs to sleep approximately one third of its life. While we sleep our brain cleans itself during the day, brain connections strengthen and increase. During sleep, connections diminish but proportions are maintained. Sleep is the price we pay for plasticity.

Panel 17: MEMORY - WHERE TIME'S ARROW IS BENT

Our brain has the remarkable ability to remember past happenings. When one thinks about what one did yesterday, time's arrow is bent into a loop. Memory underlies everything we do and is what makes the awareness of time possible.

Panel 18: So, What is Time?

Time is the dimension that accounts for change. Our universe is a "developing" universe, as are all the creatures that populate it. Time, together with space, forms spacetime, a physical entity modified by the state of motion and energy/mass.

Panel 19: What is time for?

Every sincere human exploration desires and aspires to the truth. To know the truth about something involves analytical understanding but also an exploration of its purpose within the big picture. This exhibit invites you to open the question: what is time for?

Panel 20: A Question for You

"Time is the place of God's patience: it gives us time to understand who we are, what our heart desires deep down, what we are here for, and where our happiness truly lies. Time is the space God needs to respect our freedom." - Franco Nembrini on Dante's Purgatory
